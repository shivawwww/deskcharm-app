Human in the loop · on-premise · Arabic and English

Every emergency call, understood in seconds, decided by people.

The AI Emergency Call Co-Pilot sits beside each call-taker in the Command and Control Centre. It transcribes, listens for critical cues, finds the location and pre-fills the CAD record. The call-taker accepts, edits or rejects every field.

How it works

From “hello” to a pre-filled CAD record

Six stages run in parallel on every call. Rules, not AI, choose the next protocol question, and nothing reaches CAD until the call-taker accepts it.

1 · Call arrives

Audio fork, caller number and handset location enter once.

2 · Speech

Streaming transcript, Arabic dialect and code-switch detection, quality score.

3 · Understand

Incident type, people, injuries, hazards and critical cues.

4 · Locate

Spoken place matched to the gazetteer, fused with handset position.

5 · Guide

Deterministic SOP rules pick the next question and tick the checklist.

6 · Confirm

Call-taker accepts, edits or rejects. Accepted fields go to CAD and the audit log.

Who uses it

One system, four roles

 

Call-taker

Stays on the call, not on the keyboard.

  • Live transcript with cue words highlighted
  • One-click accept, edit or reject per field
  • Next protocol question in Arabic or English

Shift supervisor

Sees the whole floor at once.

  • Wall of active calls sorted by priority
  • Region, case number and unacknowledged cues
  • Silent-call flag and per-feature kill switch

Safety board / auditor

Controls when AI is allowed to assist.

  • Shadow mode scoring against call-taker entries
  • Tamper-evident audit chain with verification
  • Written gates between rollout phases

Integrator / IT

Plugs into what the centre already runs.

  • CAD adapters, ASR gateway, REST and WebSocket API
  • Gazetteer, facility and SOP data as plain JSON
  • On-premise, no data leaves the centre
Safety and trust

Designed so a wrong suggestion cannot dispatch anything

 

Human decides

AI only proposes. Accepted fields are never overwritten; new AI values appear as an alternative.

Shadow mode first

The AI listens and is scored before it is allowed to show anything, and writes nothing to CAD.

Kill switches

Switch off extraction, location, cues, severity, SOP or CAD writes. The call-taker works exactly as today.

Audit chain

Every transcript line, suggestion, cue and decision is HMAC-chained. Edits or deletions are detected.

Low quality is flagged

Poor audio lowers confidence and is marked for verification instead of being trusted silently.

On-premise

Designed for in-country deployment with access control and encrypted storage.

Deployment, security and compliance

On-premise first, built for audit

Design options from the product concept. The MVP in this repository is a single-process reference implementation; the topologies below are the target architecture.

Single centre, on-premise

One GPU cluster in the centre's data centre, N+1 GPUs, hot standby for the orchestrator.

National, two sites

Active-active clusters; calls fail over with the telephony; shared model registry.

Hub and spoke

A central GPU hub serves regional centres over a secure WAN, with local fallback to transcript-only mode.

Sovereign cloud

In-country government cloud with dedicated GPUs, where the client's policy allows it.

Security controls

  • No audio or text leaves the centre by default; optional AI services are opt-in and audited
  • TLS 1.3 and SRTP in transit, AES-256 at rest with HSM-backed keys (target)
  • Role- and attribute-based access, four-eyes approval for exports (target)
  • Immutable, signed audit log of every suggestion and decision (in MVP)
  • Hardened containers, signed images, versioned and signed models (target)

Privacy and standards

  • Retention policies per data type with automatic deletion and legal hold (target)
  • Designed to align with UAE and KSA data-protection law, and GDPR for EU-adjacent markets
  • High-risk AI use case under the EU AI Act: human oversight, logging and documentation built in
  • Target management systems: ISO 27001, ISO 22301, ISO/IEC 42001, EENA and NENA guidance
These are design intentions. No certification is claimed.

Graceful degradation

  • LLM tier down: transcription and extraction continue
  • Extraction down: transcript only
  • All AI down: the call-taker works exactly as today
  • Kill switch per feature, owned by the shift supervisor

Commercial options and next steps

From proof of concept to production

The first commercial step is a proof of concept that secures access to real call audio under a data agreement, because dialect accuracy depends on it.

Commercial models (options)

  • Perpetual licence + support: per call-taker position plus 18–22% annual support
  • Subscription: per position per year, hardware separate
  • Managed service: per position per month including GPUs and operations
  • Add-on module inside a wider command-centre or smart-city bid

Proof-of-concept plan

  • Validate with 2–3 emergency centres and one CAD vendor
  • Dialect benchmark: public Arabic data plus 50 hours of scripted role-play calls
  • Prototype streaming ASR and extraction on one GPU; measure latency end to end
  • Agree scope, success criteria and the data-agreement template

Roadmap

Three phases, two safety gates

Each gate needs written sign-off from the operational safety board.

PHASE 1 · MVP

Assist the call-taker built

  • Transcript, dialect and code-switch detection
  • Incident type, cues, entities, severity with reasons
  • Location, regions, facilities and case record
  • SOP guidance, CAD pre-fill, audit, shadow mode
PHASE 2 · PRODUCTION

Dispatch and scale planned

  • Unit recommendation and multi-agency routing
  • CCTV auto-open, text-to-112 and chat
  • Translation with synthetic voice, duplicate and hoax detection
  • Major-incident mode, QA scoring of every call
PHASE 3 · ADVANCED

Fuse and train planned

  • ANPR, IoT and acoustic sensor correlation
  • Whisper capture, eCall, sign-language relay
  • AI first response for queued callers
  • Training simulator with role-play callers
